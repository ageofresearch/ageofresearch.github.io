Archive method

Formal proof is powerful evidence. It is not every kind of evidence.

Formagization keeps technical acceptance, semantic interpretation, reproduction, expert review, and novelty assessment separate. The archive records evidence; its maintainers do not certify mathematics by fiat.

The evidence vector

Each dimension answers a different question.

An artifact can be strong in one dimension and unresolved in another without contradiction.

DimensionQuestionTypical evidence
Source captureWhat exactly was selected for formalization?Snapshot, canonical URL, access date, normalized hash
Clean buildCan the artifact be reconstructed in its pinned environment?Fresh checkout, dependency pins, build transcript
Kernel / checkerDoes the formal proposition pass the proof system’s trusted checker?Kernel acceptance, secondary checkers, axiom report
Axioms / trusted baseWhat foundations and external components must be trusted?Axiom output, unsafe-feature scan, checker and certificate inventory
Semantic alignmentDoes the formal theorem mean what the source claim intended?Statement mapping and independent line-by-line review
Proof-assistant reviewIs the implementation technically sound and maintainable?Expert code and library review
Subject-matter reviewAre definitions, conventions, and mathematical scope appropriate?Domain-specialist report
Independent reproductionHas an external person obtained the recorded result?Environment, commands, output, and reviewer identity
NoveltyWhat is new relative to the mathematical literature?Literature search and scoped priority analysis
Core distinction Kernel acceptance is a relation between formal syntax, definitions, foundations, and a checker. Semantic alignment is a relation between that formal object and intended mathematics.

Why there is no “verified” badge

A single green label encourages readers to infer more than the underlying evidence establishes. A compiled theorem may still formalize a weakened proposition. A semantically faithful theorem may not be novel. A novel result may not yet have been independently reproduced.

The archive publishes the strongest warranted statement in each dimension and leaves the other dimensions visibly unresolved.

Publication principles

  1. 01

    Statements before tactics

    Each artifact maps informal objects, quantifiers, hypotheses, conclusions, and boundary conventions to formal declarations before celebrating proof completion.

  2. 02

    Reproducibility is part of the artifact

    Compiler versions, library commits, manifests, commands, and expected audit output travel with the proof source.

  3. 03

    The trusted base is disclosed

    Kernel acceptance is reported together with axioms, external checkers, unsafe features, certificates, and any nonstandard trusted components.

  4. 04

    AI participation is explicit

    Models are development tools, not accountable authors or independent reviewers. A human maintainer remains responsible for publication.

  5. 05

    Reviews are attributable and scoped

    A report identifies who reviewed what, against which artifact version, using which evidence, with which conflicts of interest.

  6. 06

    Corrections are append-only

    Statement-changing corrections receive new versions. Previous releases, failed reproductions, disputes, and withdrawals remain visible.

What inclusion means

Inclusion means that an artifact meets the archive’s structural, disclosure, and reproducibility requirements. It does not establish publication priority, correctness of the source, semantic equivalence, subject-matter endorsement, or novelty unless those states are separately supported by recorded evidence.

Natural-language proofs, LLM transcripts, finite experiments, and unreviewed theorem translations can be valuable source material. None is itself a formal proof artifact.

System scope

The archive is Lean-first rather than Lean-exclusive. A submission may use Isabelle, Rocq/Coq, HOL, Agda, Mizar, SAT/SMT certificates, or another checker, provided its trusted base and reproduction route are explicit.
