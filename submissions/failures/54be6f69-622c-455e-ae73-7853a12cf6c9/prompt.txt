## The conjecture

I selected **Conjecture 1.2** from Gray, Payne, Swisher, and Watson’s *Fixed perimeter analogues of some partition results*, published in *Discrete Mathematics* in 2026.

For a partition \(\pi\), define its perimeter by

\[
\operatorname{per}(\pi)
=
\text{largest part}
+
\text{number of parts}
-1.
\]

Let:

\[
\FO_{j,k}(n)
\]

count perimeter-\(n\) partitions having exactly \(j\) **present part sizes divisible by \(k\)**, and let

\[
\FD_{j,k}(n)
\]

count perimeter-\(n\) partitions having exactly \(j\) **part sizes occurring at least \(k\) times**.

Their conjecture states that, for every fixed \(j\geq0\) and \(k\geq2\),

\[
\FD_{j,k}(n)\geq\FO_{j,k}(n)
\]

for all sufficiently large \(n\). The paper proves equality when \(k=2\), but leaves the general case as Conjecture 1.2. citeturn675832view0turn707962search3

## Result

The conjecture is true. In fact:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\FO_{j,k}(n)}{\FD_{j,k}(n)}
\longrightarrow 0
\qquad
(k\geq3)
}
\]

for every fixed \(j\).

Thus the conjectured weak inequality can be strengthened to

\[
\FD_{j,k}(n)>\FO_{j,k}(n)
\]

for all sufficiently large \(n\) whenever \(k>2\).

For \(k=2\), the two functions remain exactly equal for every \(n\), as already known.

## Core proof

Introduce the bivariate generating functions

\[
\mathcal O_k(z,q)
=
\sum_{n\geq1}\sum_{j\geq0}
\FO_{j,k}(n)z^jq^n,
\]

\[
\mathcal D_k(z,q)
=
\sum_{n\geq1}\sum_{j\geq0}
\FD_{j,k}(n)z^jq^n.
\]

Writing a partition through its multiplicities and summing over its largest part gives the rational functions

\[
\mathcal O_k(z,q)
=
\frac{S_k(q)+zq^k}
     {P_O(q)-zq^{k+1}},
\]

\[
\mathcal D_k(z,q)
=
\frac{q(1-q^{k-1})+zq^k}
     {P_D(q)-zq^{k+1}},
\]

where

\[
S_k(q)
=
\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}q^r(1-q)^{k-r},
\]

\[
P_O(q)
=
(1-q)^k-q^k+q^{k+1},
\qquad
P_D(q)
=
1-2q+q^{k+1}.
\]

The denominators factor as

\[
P_D(q)
=
(1-q)A_D(q),
\qquad
A_D(q)
=
1-q-q^2-\cdots-q^k,
\]

and

\[
P_O(q)
=
(1-q)A_O(q),
\qquad
A_O(q)
=
(1-q)^{k-1}-q^k.
\]

Extracting the coefficient of \(z^j\) gives, for \(j\geq1\),

\[
\sum_{n\geq1}\FD_{j,k}(n)q^n
=
\frac{q^{(k+1)j-1}}
     {(1-q)^{j-1}A_D(q)^{j+1}},
\]

and

\[
\sum_{n\geq1}\FO_{j,k}(n)q^n
=
\frac{q^{(k+1)j-1}R_k(q)}
     {(1-q)^{j-1}A_O(q)^{j+1}},
\]

where

\[
R_k(q)
=
(1-q)^{k-2}
+
\sum_{s=2}^{k-1}
q^s(1-q)^{k-1-s}.
\]

Every term of \(R_k(q)\) is positive for \(0<q<1\).

For \(j=0\), the corresponding formulas are

\[
\sum_{n\geq1}\FD_{0,k}(n)q^n
=
\frac{q(1+q+\cdots+q^{k-2})}{A_D(q)}

and

\[
\sum_{n\geq1}\FO_{0,k}(n)q^n
=
\frac{T_k(q)}{A_O(q)},

with

\[
T_k(q)
=
\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}q^r(1-q)^{k-r-1}>0
\qquad (0<q<1).
\]

Therefore the asymptotic growth is governed by the smallest-modulus zeros of \(A_D\) and \(A_O\).

Let their positive zeros be \(\rho_D\) and \(\rho_O\). Both lie in \((1/2,1)\). Each is also the unique zero of minimum modulus:

For \(A_D\), a zero satisfies

\[
z+z^2+\cdots+z^k=1.
\]

The triangle inequality rules out \(|z|<\rho_D\), and equality on \(|z|=\rho_D\) forces \(z\) to be positive real.

For \(A_O\), a zero satisfies

\[
(1-z)^{k-1}=z^k.
\]

The inequality

\[
|1-z|\geq1-|z|
\]

similarly rules out \(|z|<\rho_O\), and equality on the boundary again forces \(z\) to be positive real.

The decisive comparison is

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_O(q)-A_D(q)
&=
(1-q)^{k-1}-1+q+q^2+\cdots+q^{k-1}\\
&=
q\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}
\left(q^\ell-(1-q)^\ell\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(q>1/2\) and \(k\geq3\), this quantity is strictly positive. Since \(\rho_D>1/2\),

\[
A_O(\rho_D)>A_D(\rho_D)=0.

Because \(A_O\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,1)\), its zero occurs later:

\[
\boxed{\rho_D<\rho_O}.
\]

\[
\FD_{j,k}(n)
\sim
C_D(j,k)n^{e_j}\rho_D^{-n},

\[
\FO_{j,k}(n)
\sim
C_O(j,k)n^{e_j}\rho_O^{-n},

where \(e_0=0\) and \(e_j=j\) for \(j\geq1\). Consequently,

\[
\frac{\FO_{j,k}(n)}{\FD_{j,k}(n)}
\sim
\frac{C_O(j,k)}{C_D(j,k)}
\left(\frac{\rho_D}{\rho_O}\right)^n
\longrightarrow0.
\]
